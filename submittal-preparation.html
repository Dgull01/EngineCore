Scope, stated plainly

Does it need a PE stamp?

This is the first question every contractor asks, so here is the answer before you fill out anything.

Usually not. Pre-engineered kitchen suppression systems are listed assemblies. Most jurisdictions accept the manufacturer's factory drawings plus a licensed installer — not a sealed engineering design. Where your AHJ does require a seal, EngineCore identifies that requirement in the first review, before preparation is authorized, and coordinates with your engineer of record.

What EngineCore does

  • Prepare complete, code-correct submittal packages ready to hand to the AHJ
  • Produce a scaled piping arrangement with a derived 30° reviewer view, verified against the controlling manufacturer DIM
  • Cross-check appliance schedules, nozzle listings, coverage and flow points
  • Reconcile a physical-parts ledger against what the drawing actually calls for
  • Assemble cut sheets, UL listings and completed jurisdiction forms
  • Deliver it all under your title block, your sheet numbering and your preparer's name
  • Write the responses when a reviewer returns comments

Outside this scope

  • Seal drawings or act as engineer of record
  • Perform the installation or certify work the reviewer did not witness
  • Pull your permit or submit under your license
  • Promise an approval — that decision belongs to the jurisdiction
